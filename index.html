01 / Meet the material

Digital design.
A handmade feeling.

Claymorphism gives interface elements the appearance of soft, molded objects. It brings a sense of volume to a flat screen without needing real 3D rendering.

The effect comes from solid color, generous curves, soft outer shadows, and subtle inner shading. Used selectively, those details draw attention to the things visitors can do.

01 A solid surface
Opaque colors give each object a clear silhouette and keep the content readable.
02 Light with direction
A gentle highlight and a deeper shaded edge make the surface feel rounded.
03 Depth with a purpose
Raised actions invite interaction while quieter text areas leave room to read.

04 / Behind the softness

The style is playful.
The thinking is practical.

A distinctive surface is only one part of a useful website. I shape the design around what your visitors need to understand and do.

  1. Start with your brand

    Define your audience, content, and main action before choosing colors or adding depth.

  2. Shape the important moments

    Use clay for key actions and featured content. Give longer text and detailed information a quieter treatment.

  3. Adapt to the screen

    Reflow the layout for smaller screens, simplify decorative layers, and leave comfortable space for touch controls.

  4. Refine the experience

    Check readability, keyboard access, focus states, reduced motion, and loading behavior before launch.

05 / A few useful answers

Is clay right
for your website?

Does everything have to look soft and rounded?

No. A clay button, a featured panel, or one sculpted illustration may be enough. The rest of the page can stay simple, with conventional navigation and clear typography.

Can the design feel professional?

Yes. Restrained colors, consistent lighting, and disciplined spacing can keep it composed. The right amount of softness depends on your audience and the tone your brand needs.

How does it work on mobile?

The layout should reorganize around the content. Decorative overlaps can be removed, shadows reduced, and controls kept large enough to use comfortably.

Does Claymorphism need heavy 3D libraries?

No. Many clay effects can be built with standard CSS backgrounds, borders, and shadows. Complex illustrations may need assets, but the interface itself can stay lightweight.

When would a simpler style work better?

Dense dashboards, long forms, and information-heavy pages often benefit from flatter surfaces. Clay can still highlight an important action without adding visual weight to every element.
